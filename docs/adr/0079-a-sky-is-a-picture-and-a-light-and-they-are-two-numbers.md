# ADR 0079 — A sky is a picture and a light, and they are two numbers

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-08-19 · **Builds on:** ADR 0034, ADR 0049 ·
**Implements:** item 12c of `docs/13-the-engine-gate.md`

## Context

ADR 0049 made an environment map do two jobs at once. It is **drawn**, by the sky pass, as the
backdrop behind everything. And it is **integrated**, at load, into an irradiance map and a
prefiltered specular chain that light every surface in the world. One image, one brightness, two
completely different roles.

`games/atrium` is where that stopped working, and the failure is worth stating exactly because it
looks like a tuning problem and is not one.

The room's `.hdr` is generated by `cargo run -p atrium --bin daylight`. It was authored with an
overall `SCALE` of `0.34` — a number arrived at honestly, by looking: anything higher and the
environment overwhelmed the `DirectionalLight`, washing the room into a flat grey with no sun in it.
`0.34` is a **correct fill value** and it produces the cool-shade-against-warm-sun colour separation
that is the whole point of image-based lighting.

At `0.34` the sky itself is a dark slate field. A daylight sky seen through an oculus, in a room
where the sun is casting hard shadows, is **darker than the sunlit floor beneath it** — you are
looking directly at the light source and it is dimmer than the thing it is lighting.

**There is no value that is right for both**, because they are not the same quantity. Turning the
number up to fix the backdrop breaks the fill; turning it down to fix the fill breaks the backdrop.

## Decision

### `Environment::sky_ambient` scales the ambient contribution and nothing else

A single `f32`, defaulting to `1.0`, multiplied into `ambient_diffuse + ambient_specular` in
`mesh.wgsl`. The sky pass never reads it, so the drawn backdrop is always the map as authored.

The `.hdr` therefore holds **the sky's real colour** and the look holds **how much of it bounces
back in**. `atrium_daylight.hdr` went to `SCALE 1.0` and `atrium.environment` gained
`sky_ambient 0.34`, so the fill is arithmetically identical to what it was and only the picture
changed.

### Every comparable engine ships this split, independently

- **Unity** — the skybox material's exposure is separate from Environment Lighting's *Intensity
  Multiplier*.
- **Unreal** — the sky's own brightness is separate from the Sky Light's *Intensity Scale*.
- **Godot** — `Sky.sky_energy_multiplier` is separate from `Environment.ambient_light_energy`.

Three engines, three independent designs, same seam. That is about as strong a signal as engine
design offers, and it is why this is one field rather than a workaround.

### It rides in the view uniform as its own `vec4`

`GpuMeshView::ambient_params`, `x` used. `fog_params` had three free lanes and would have cost
nothing — ADR 0078 §2 refused the same saving for the same reason, and this is a *per-view* uniform
written a handful of times a frame, so sixteen bytes is not a constraint anybody can measure.

## Consequences

*(Live measurements deliberately absent — see the rule this ADR's session added to `docs/07`: an ADR
records a decision, not a measurement. The numbers live in `docs/13` §1.)*

- **`1.0` is the identity, so no existing `.environment` changed** and every capture stayed
  byte-identical. `games/scarp` and `games/warren` still bake their fill into their maps, and are
  deliberately left alone: neither has a sky anyone can see, so there is nothing to gain.
- **It made a diagnosis possible that was not possible before, and immediately overturned one.** The
  Atrium had a dark band across the top of the frame that the engine gate flagged as unlit ceiling. I
  argued it was the sky, and offered as proof that recolouring the map magenta turned the band
  magenta. **That experiment cannot distinguish the two hypotheses**, because recolouring the map
  moves the backdrop and the fill *together* — which is the exact coupling this ADR exists to break.
  Setting `sky_ambient 0.0` separates them in one capture: the band went black, so it is a surface.
  The original reading was right and mine was wrong.
- **The lesson is more general than the field.** A single knob feeding two consumers cannot be used
  to tell those consumers apart, so any experiment that moves it is uninformative about which one
  responded. That is worth recognising *before* reaching for it, because such an experiment produces
  a confident, wrong answer rather than no answer.
- **`tests/capture.rs` pins both halves.** Two captures at two fill values: the backdrop pixel must
  be byte-identical and the surface pixel must not be. Either assertion alone passes for the wrong
  reason — a field that did nothing satisfies the first, a field wired into the sky pass satisfies
  the second — and a `> 60` guard keeps the first from passing against a black corner.
- **It does not fix the Atrium's view, and that is a separate finding.** The room is 20 × 20 × 4 m
  and the follow camera sits at y = 3.69, thirty-one centimetres below the roof. The oculus is
  therefore **not visible from anywhere the player can stand**, which is why every upward capture is
  flat grey. The best image this project has produced was taken by moving the camera in the scene
  file. That is a room-proportions problem, recorded here so the two are not confused.
